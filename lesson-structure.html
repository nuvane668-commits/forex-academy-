Market Structure

Learn how price creates highs and lows, how structure changes, and how traders identify BOS and CHOCH.

What Is Market Structure?

Market structure is the way price moves through a series of highs and lows. By studying these swings, a trader can describe whether the market is generally moving upward, downward, or changing direction.

Simple idea:

Don't look at every candle separately. Step back and look at the important highs and lows that price is creating.

What Are HH and HL?

HH — Higher High

A new swing high that forms above the previous important swing high.

HL — Higher Low

A pullback that stops above the previous important swing low.

LH — Lower High

A swing high that forms below the previous important swing high.

LL — Lower Low

A new swing low that forms below the previous important swing low.

Understanding BOS

BOS means Break of Structure. In this educational example, price moves upward and breaks above a previous significant swing high. That break is marked on the chart as BOS.

1

Price creates a swing high.

2

Price pulls back and forms a higher low.

3

Price moves upward again and breaks the previous swing high.

4

That break is identified as a possible break of structure.

Why Structure Matters

Understanding structure helps a trader avoid looking at isolated candles without context. Instead, the trader can ask whether price is consistently creating higher highs and higher lows, or lower highs and lower lows.

Important:

A BOS is not automatically a trading signal. Traders may use additional information such as liquidity, displacement, order blocks, fair value gaps, risk management and higher-timeframe context before considering a setup.

Practical Market Structure Chart

This chart shows how traders can identify market structure and combine BOS, CHOCH, liquidity, Order Blocks and Fair Value Gaps in a practical setup.

How to read the setup:
  1. Price creates HH → HL → HH, showing bullish structure.
  2. A previous high is broken — this is the BOS.
  3. Price takes liquidity before making a strong displacement.
  4. The displacement leaves an FVG.
  5. The final bearish candle before the bullish displacement is treated as the Bullish Order Block.
  6. Price retraces into the OB/FVG area.
  7. A trader may look for confirmation before entering.
  8. The stop loss belongs beyond the invalidation area, while the target can be placed near a previous high/liquidity area.
Important: This is an educational example, not a guaranteed trade signal. The exact identification of BOS, CHOCH, liquidity, OB and FVG depends on the timeframe and the trader's rules.

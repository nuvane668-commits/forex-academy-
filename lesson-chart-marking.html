CHART ANALYSIS

Chart Marking Academy

Learn how to turn a raw price chart into a structured analysis. Identify market structure, BOS, CHOCH, FVG, liquidity and other price-action concepts using clear rules instead of guessing.

🎯 The Goal

Chart marking is not about covering a chart with dozens of lines. The objective is to identify the few pieces of information that actually matter to your trading plan.

Our analysis sequence:
Context → Structure → Liquidity → Location → Trigger → Risk → Entry

1. Market Structure

Market structure describes how price is moving from one swing to another.

HH
Higher High — price creates a high above the previous significant high.
HL
Higher Low — price holds above the previous significant low.
LH
Lower High — price fails to create a higher high.
LL
Lower Low — price creates a low below the previous significant low.

Notice the sequence: HH → HL → HH → HL. That describes an example of bullish structure.

2. BOS — Break of Structure

A Break of Structure is a structural break that can indicate price has continued in the direction of the existing structure.

When marking BOS, first identify the relevant swing point. Then determine whether price has actually broken the level according to your chosen rules.

Important: A wick through a level does not automatically mean that every trader should call it a BOS. Define your confirmation rule before using the concept.

3. CHOCH — Change of Character

CHOCH is commonly used by traders to describe a potential change in the character or direction of price structure.

The key word is potential. A CHOCH is not a guarantee that a complete trend reversal will follow. Look for additional context and confirmation.

4. FVG — Fair Value Gap

An FVG is commonly described as an imbalance between candles in a three-candle sequence. Traders often mark the area between the relevant candle wicks as a zone of interest.

The exact rules used to identify an FVG should be defined in your strategy. Not every visually interesting gap is automatically a high-quality trade.

5. Liquidity

Liquidity is often discussed around obvious areas where many traders may place stops or pending orders, such as previous highs, previous lows and equal highs or lows.

A liquidity sweep is not automatically a trade signal. It needs to be interpreted within the broader market context.

6. Order Blocks

Order block terminology varies between trading communities. In this Academy, we will define the exact rules before asking students to mark one.

Academy rule: Don't mark an order block simply because a candle looks large or interesting. Define the preceding structure, displacement and the specific zone you intend to use.

7. Premium & Discount

Take a clearly defined price range and divide it around the midpoint. The upper portion is commonly called premium and the lower portion discount.

🧩 Put Everything Together

Professional chart analysis is not about finding one magic concept. It is about building a logical chain of evidence.

1. Context

What is the broader market environment?

2. Structure

HH, HL, LH, LL, range or transition?

3. Liquidity

Where are important highs and lows?

4. Location

Is price at an area relevant to the plan?

5. Trigger

What specific event confirms the idea?

6. Risk

Where is the idea invalidated?
